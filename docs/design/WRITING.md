# Writing guide

How every word in the product should read: marketing pages, the app, admin, emails, errors, PDFs and share pages. The goal is copy that sounds like a person who knows local businesses wrote it, not a template or an AI. Wording choices for product terms are in MVP_SPEC 8.4; this guide covers tone, punctuation and style. [Back to design](./DESIGN.md)

## Punctuation

- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere (the long dashes). Use a comma, period, colon or parentheses instead. Ranges use "to": "5 to 10 competitors".
- One exclamation mark at most per page, and only where a person would really use one (a completed setup, not a feature list).
- Straight, simple sentences. Prefer two short sentences over one long one joined by semicolons.
- Numbers as digits ("3 AI assistants", "12 questions"). Money with the symbol and no trailing zeros ("$149 a month").
- Sentence case for headings, buttons and menu items ("Run scan", not "Run Scan").
- No ellipses except on a loading state ("Scanning…").

## Words and tone

- Say what the product does in plain words. A local business owner with no marketing background must understand every sentence.
- Speak to the reader as "you". Talk about the product as "we" only where a person would.
- Concrete over clever: "AI recommended you in 5 of 10 questions" beats "Unlock your AI visibility potential".
- Never promise results we cannot control ("rank #1 in ChatGPT", "guaranteed"). Scores are mention rates, never ranks (D-63).
- No filler openers or closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Look no further", "In conclusion", "Whether you're X or Y".
- Avoid words that read as AI-written or salesy: unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, seamless, seamlessly, robust, leverage, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionize, supercharge, delve, navigate the landscape, in the realm of, a testament to, tapestry, harness, streamline, next-level, effortless, world-class.
- No rule-of-three padding ("fast, simple and powerful") unless all three are true and specific.
- No emojis in the product or emails.
- Errors say what happened and what to do next, without blame: "We couldn't reach that website. Check the address and try again."
- Empty states tell the user the next step, in one line.

## Checks before merging copy

1. Search the change for long dashes (U+2014, U+2013) and the banned words above.
2. Read every changed string out loud once. If it sounds like an ad or a robot, rewrite it.
3. Check buttons and headings are sentence case and that every number is a digit.
